SEAR Lab Industrial, Manufacturing & Systems Engineering · UT Arlington

Twenty-eight things you will be able to do, and prove you can.

Most doctoral programs leave research skills to osmosis. You are expected to absorb version control, peer review, reproducibility and proposal writing from the air around you — and the gaps surface at the worst possible moment. A student who cannot reproduce their own Chapter 3. A submission that stalls on formatting. Here, each of those skills is explicit, demonstrated once on your own dissertation work, and then yours permanently.

The curriculum

Twenty-eight skills in six milestones. You choose what to work on and when — nothing is locked, and a first-year who needs the review cycle can take the review cycle. Each skill is performed as one week's ordinary research-log entry, so it replaces work rather than stacking on top of it. Two milestones a semester finishes the set in three to four semesters, which lands around when you propose.

How the week runs

Every week you keep a research log in three channels, and it is graded every week. At the end of a semester you read back across the whole thing — which is the point, because progress in research is invisible at the scale of a single week and obvious at the scale of fifteen.

Reading

One paper, chapter, or set of conference papers. An annotated entry that goes past summary to what the evidence actually supports.

Writing

A page. Draft prose, an outline, a plan, a reflection — anything that moves the research forward on the page rather than in your head.

Arithmetic

What you computed, what broke, what you are doing next. Where the code, the models and the solver work get written down.

What we work on

  • Energy systems optimizationCapacity expansion and dispatch across ERCOT; ReEDS; large-scale MILP in GAMS, CPLEX and Gurobi.
  • Battery materials and recyclingMaterial flows, recovery pathways, and what recycling actually displaces.
  • Critical minerals supply chainsMixed-integer models of sourcing, processing and vulnerability under disruption.
  • Transportation decarbonizationFleet and freight transitions, and the grid consequences that follow them.
  • Surrogate modelingCheap stand-ins for expensive solves, and knowing when to distrust them.

The lab runs three tiers — doctoral research, master's team projects, and undergraduate REU and UROP students. By your third year you are supervising, which is why mentoring is a graded skill here and not a thing you are assumed to pick up.

What we look for

  • You can say what you do not know. The single most useful thing in a first-year student, and the rarest.
  • You finish things. A small finished study beats an ambitious unfinished one, every time.
  • You use AI and check it. Not using it is a disadvantage. Trusting it without verification is worse.
  • You write. Not well, necessarily. Regularly.

What we do not require: prior optimization coursework, a background in energy systems, or having touched a terminal before. Those are what the first two milestones are for.
