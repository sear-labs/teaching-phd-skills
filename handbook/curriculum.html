M1 The Workbench

Year 1 · 5 skills

The five surfaces you actually work on, plus the habit that makes all of them safe.

01

Spreadsheet discipline

arithmetic 3 hours prereq: none

Spreadsheets are where most lab data first lands and where most silent errors are born: a formula dragged one row short, a sort that moved one column and not its neighbours, a constant typed into a cell six months ago that nobody can now explain. Reviewers never see the spreadsheet — they see the number it produced.

Turn in .xlsx plus the annotation, in a WA entry

Passes when — 6 checks
  • Raw sheet exists and no formula writes to it
  • At least one named range used in a formula
  • No bare numeric constant inside a formula except 0, 1, and documented unit conversions
  • One pivot answering a stated question; one chart with axes labelled and units given
  • An AI-generated formula, with every argument annotated in the student's own words
  • A deliberate break, with the prediction shown before the result
02

Notebooks and AI assistance

arithmetic 4 hours prereq: none

The failure mode is not a bad answer. It is a plausible one.

Turn in notebook link (view access) plus the verification table, in a WA entry

Passes when — 7 checks
  • Link opens for someone who is not the author
  • First cell shows interpreter version and pinned package versions; seed set
  • The hand-computed answer appears before the code that recomputes it
  • Any divergence reconciled in writing, not removed
  • Three structurally different prompts, not rewordings
  • Every factual claim is a row, each naming a specific verification method
  • At least one claim identified as wrong or unverifiable
03

The command line and a remote machine

arithmetic 5 hours prereq: none

This lab runs Linux servers alongside Windows workstations, and the jobs that matter — a ReEDS run, a large MILP, a parameter sweep — do not belong on your laptop. They outlive your battery, your commute, and the moment your machine decides to install an update.

Turn in terminal transcript plus retrieved output, in a WA entry

Passes when — 5 checks
  • Local: navigation, a pipe, a redirect, and a recursive search, all shown
  • ssh to a lab server or HPC node, shown
  • A file moved to the remote machine and a result moved back
  • A job that survived a disconnection, with evidence it did — the submission, then the output
  • The log read, with one line quoted that you had to look up
04

LaTeX and Overleaf

writing 4 hours prereq: 15-imrad, 16-citation-verification

Submissions stall on formatting, and they stall late — at the point where the science is done and the only thing between you and a submitted paper is a template you have never opened. The second failure is quieter: a journal template is a contract about length, and a draft written in Word at 1.15 spacing has no idea whether it fits.

Turn in Overleaf link plus compiled PDF

Passes when — 5 checks
  • The template is the journal's official one, named
  • Compiles with zero errors
  • Warnings read, and any overfull box in a figure or table resolved
  • Bibliography generated from a .bib file, not hand-written
  • Page count stated against the journal's limit
05

Sanity checks and orders of magnitude

arithmetic 3 hours prereq: none

The errors that survive code review are the ones where the code is correct and the number is wrong. A diff will not catch a capacity in MW that should have been GW, a price in $/tonne read as $/kg, or a discount rate entered as 7 instead of 0.07. The model runs, converges, reports a clean objective, and the answer is off by a thousand.

Turn in text entry in a WA entry

Passes when — 6 checks
  • Predicted magnitude written before the run, with a timestamp or commit that proves it
  • Units stated for every input and output involved
  • One published reference value, cited with a source, and the comparison
  • Any discrepancy over a factor of two explained, not waved through
  • One extreme case run and its behaviour described
  • If a solver was used: status word, achieved gap and residual all reported — or an explicit note that no solver was involved

M2 Reproducible Code

Year 1 · 4 skills

The milestone that prevents "I cannot reproduce my own Chapter 3." All four are performed on your actual dissertation repository — not a sample, not a copy.

06

Git and GitHub

arithmetic 4 hours prereq: none

Do this one before anything else in M2. Git history is permanent. A credential committed once is compromised even if the next commit removes it, even if you force-push, even if the repo was private at the time — and the fix is rotating the credential, not amending the commit.

Turn in repo URL plus pasted git check-ignore -v output

Passes when — 5 checks
  • Repo URL resolves
  • git check-ignore -v output pasted, showing a matched rule for every data and credential path
  • Searching the full history for your own credential variable names returns nothing
  • At least five commits whose messages state a reason
  • git status on a clean checkout is empty
07

Code folder structure

arithmetic 4 hours prereq: 06-git-github

"I cannot reproduce my own Chapter 3" is the concrete failure this prevents, and it has a specific mechanism: processed data overwrote raw data, or a script's output became its own input, and now there is no path back to the numbers in the draft. It surfaces at the worst possible moment — during revisions, under a deadline, when the person who could explain the directory is you eight months ago.

Turn in repo URL plus one paragraph on what moved where

Passes when — 4 checks
  • Repo layout matches the archetype
  • Nothing under the raw tier is written by any script (verify, do not assert)
  • README is usable by someone who has never seen the project
  • Two places the standard is stricter than Wilson et al., identified and explained
08

Reproducible environments

arithmetic 4 hours (plus your partner's 1 hour) prereq: 07-code-folder-structure

There is exactly one test of whether an environment is reproducible, and it is not a file listing. It is: a labmate ran it and got your number. Everything else is a proxy.

Turn in repo URL plus your partner's signed run log

Passes when — 5 checks
  • Partner reproduced the named figure on their own machine, or the divergence is explained
  • Stated tolerance appears before the comparison, not after
  • Solver version and interpreter version both recorded in the repo
  • Every question the partner had to ask is listed as a README defect
  • README updated in response
09

CLI agents and Claude Code

arithmetic 3 hours prereq: 06-git-github, 02-ai-prompting

An agent with filesystem access is fast, confident, and wrong often enough to matter — and a research repo is the most expensive place for that combination, because a plausible edit to an analysis script produces plausible numbers. The skill being assessed is not prompting.

Turn in transcript excerpt plus the accepted diff, in a WA entry

Passes when — 5 checks
  • Task stated in one sentence, timestamped before the session
  • Work done on a branch, not the default branch
  • The full diff is shown and was reviewed line by line
  • At least one thing the agent got wrong is recorded
  • Whatever check the repo already has (tests, an agreement assertion) still passes after the merge

M3 Reading and Reasoning

Year 1-2 · 5 skills

Evaluating somebody else's argument, finding where the field stops, spotting an inference that fails, building a claim of your own, and saying it no more strongly than your evidence allows.

10

Reading and critiquing papers

reading 3 hours prereq: none

Summarising is not critiquing, and the weekly log makes the difference easy to hide. A summary restates what the authors claim.

Turn in WR entry, 300-500 words

Passes when — 4 checks
  • The central claim is quoted verbatim, with a location
  • One load-bearing assumption is named
  • The gap between what is claimed and what is shown is stated explicitly
  • A specific alternative is proposed, with its cost
11

Literature review and gap-finding

reading 8 hours prereq: 10-critiquing-papers

"No one has done X" is not a gap. It is frequently evidence that X is not worth doing, and a committee will say so.

Turn in PDF: the map plus the gap paragraph

Passes when — 7 checks
  • Fifteen papers, cited
  • Three candidate axes written down before one was chosen, each rejected one with a stated reason
  • One named axis, with units or categories stated
  • Every paper positioned on it
  • The empty region identified
  • One sentence on why it is empty, distinguishing "not yet possible" from "not worth doing"
  • The three-part sentence, written out
12

Logic and its failures

reading 3 hours prereq: 13-claim-reason-evidence

An argument can be perfectly valid and completely false, or true in every part and still invalid. Keeping those apart is the content of a first-semester logic course, and it is the tool you use to say why a paper's conclusion does not follow rather than just feeling that it does not.

Turn in text entry, three worked examples

Passes when — 5 checks
  • Three examples, one from each of the three sources
  • Each passage quoted, with a locator
  • The specific failure named, not just "this is weak"
  • A concrete repair for each — what evidence or qualifier would fix it
  • The one from your own draft is actually fixed, and the draft link shows it
13

Claim, reason, evidence

writing 3 hours prereq: none

Most students arrive with a topic and believe it is a claim. "My research is about lithium supply chains" is a topic.

Turn in text entry or PDF, one page

Passes when — 5 checks
  • A single claim sentence with all three slots filled
  • The claim is contestable — someone competent could disagree with it
  • The warrant is stated as a general principle, not restated as the evidence
  • Three objections, none of them trivial
  • For each objection, one line on whether the paper currently answers it
14

Saying exactly what you claim

writing 2 hours prereq: 13-claim-reason-evidence

Reviewers reject papers for overclaiming more often than for bad work. The sentence "our model proves that recycling reduces import dependence" will be attacked, and correctly, because a model does not prove anything about the world — it shows what follows from its assumptions.

Turn in claim-verb audit of a draft and of your own CV, plus both revised

Passes when — 7 checks
  • Every claim-making sentence in the draft appears as a row
  • Each row states what the evidence licenses, not just whether the verb "sounds strong"
  • Every instance of proves is either removed or justified as genuine mathematics
  • At least one claim gains an explicit scope condition
  • The revised draft is linked and the changes are in it
  • The same audit run over every bullet of your own CV, with the revised CV attached
  • At least one CV verb downgraded to what the evidence actually supports

M4 The Manuscript

Year 2 · 5 skills

Everything that goes into assembling a document somebody else can read, trust and cite. The argument itself was M3; this is the production of the artifact that carries it.

15

IMRAD structure

writing 3 hours prereq: none

Most rejected drafts in this lab are not rejected for the research. They are rejected because the reader cannot find the contribution — it is implied by the results section and stated nowhere.

Turn in Overleaf link

Passes when — 5 checks
  • The claim sentence exists, with both blanks filled
  • "Matters because" gives a reason other than novelty
  • One topic sentence per IMRAD section
  • Each topic sentence traceable to the claim in one step
  • Any section that failed the test is named, with the resolution stated
16

Reference manager and citation verification

reading 3 hours prereq: none

Fabricated references are a live failure mode, not a hypothetical one — a language model will produce a plausible author list, a plausible title, a plausible year, and a DOI that resolves to something else entirely. The subtler and more common failure is worse: a real paper cited for a claim it does not make.

Turn in table, PDF or text entry, plus the updated draft

Passes when — 5 checks
  • Ten rows
  • Every row has a resolving DOI or a stable URL
  • Every row locates the claim by page, section, or figure
  • Any reference that failed is struck from the draft, and the draft link shows it
  • The count of failures is stated plainly
17

Research ethics and authorship

writing 2 hours prereq: none

Authorship disputes happen at submission, which is the one moment when they cannot be resolved calmly. Everyone has been working for a year on an assumption nobody stated, and the assumptions do not match.

Turn in text entry, CRediT-style, plus evidence it was circulated

Passes when — 6 checks
  • Every contributor named, including students at other tiers
  • CRediT roles assigned per person
  • Author versus acknowledgement decided with a stated reason
  • Order justified in one sentence
  • Corresponding author named
  • Evidence of circulation
18

Figure and table design

writing 4 hours prereq: none

Figures are read first and often alone. Yours will be screenshotted into someone else's slide deck, printed in greyscale by a reviewer, and looked at for four seconds by a programme committee member deciding whether to read the abstract.

Turn in PDF showing before, after, and the standalone caption

Passes when — 6 checks
  • Before and after both shown
  • Caption comprehensible with no body text — test it on someone outside your subfield
  • Units on both axes
  • Greyscale conversion shown, series still distinguishable
  • Colour is never the only channel carrying meaning
  • Every plotted series is named in the caption
19

Accessibility of notebooks and figures

writing 3 hours prereq: 18-figures-tables

This one has a date. The DOJ's 2024 Title II rule makes WCAG 2.1 Level AA the standard for web content a covered entity makes available, and a public university is a covered entity.

Turn in repo URL

Passes when — 5 checks
  • Every figure has alt text naming the trend or takeaway, not the chart type
  • No heading level is skipped anywhere in the notebook
  • Greyscale check passed
  • Tables have header rows and are not images
  • Body text contrast measured at 4.5:1 or better, with the measurement stated

M5 The Review Cycle

Year 3+ · 5 skills

Everything between "the draft is finished" and "it is out in the world." Review someone else's work, answer a review of your own, choose where to send it, present it, and defend it under questioning.

20

Reviewing papers

reading 4 hours prereq: 10-critiquing-papers

You will be asked to review before anyone teaches you how, and the default is to produce something either useless ("interesting paper, minor comments") or destructive. Reviewing is also the fastest way to learn what editors are looking for, which makes you better at submitting.

Turn in structured referee report, PDF

Passes when — 5 checks
  • Summary written in the reviewer's own words
  • At least two strengths, each pointing at a specific section
  • At least two weaknesses, each tied to a line or section number
  • Must-fix and would-improve separated into distinct lists
  • An explicit recommendation
21

Response to reviewers

writing 4 hours prereq: 20-reviewing-papers

Rebuttals fail in two directions and both lose the paper. Defensive letters argue with the reviewer and get rejected by an editor who reads tone before substance.

Turn in PDF response letter

Passes when — 5 checks
  • Every reviewer point has a row
  • Every claimed change is located by section or line
  • At least one point respectfully declined, with a reason rather than an apology
  • The revised draft actually contains the changes claimed — spot-checked against three rows
  • No unattached apologies
22

Venue and audience selection

writing 3 hours prereq: 15-imrad

A paper sent to the wrong venue does not get rejected quickly — it gets desk-rejected after six weeks, or sent to reviewers who evaluate it against criteria it was never trying to meet. That is a lost semester.

Turn in one-page memo plus draft cover letter or outreach message

Passes when — 7 checks
  • Three named venues
  • All four attributes present for each, with sources
  • Choice justified against the two rejected, on grounds specific to this paper
  • Cover letter states the contribution in its first paragraph
  • AI-disclosure requirement identified and a draft disclosure written
  • If run on an employer: three named groups, not companies, each with the specific thing it builds
  • The recipient demonstrably works on the thing the message is about — or the ask is an introduction instead
23

Conference talk and poster

writing 6 hours prereq: 15-imrad, 18-figures-tables

A conference talk is twelve minutes and you will be asked three questions you did not anticipate. The failure is not nerves; it is that most students have never once said their contribution out loud in a single sentence, so the talk becomes a tour of the methodology and the contribution never lands.

Turn in slides PDF plus the three questions and revised answers

Passes when — 5 checks
  • Delivered within twelve minutes, plus or minus one
  • A slide carries the contribution claim, matching the manuscript word for word
  • Figures meet the skill 15 checks
  • Three questions recorded verbatim
  • A revised answer for each, and a note on whether it implies a change to the paper
24

Defending your work

writing 4 hours prereq: 13-claim-reason-evidence

A defence fails in two directions and both lose the room. You can dig in and argue with the question, which reads as defensiveness and makes the committee press harder.

Turn in three steelmanned objections with responses, delivered live to the lab

Passes when — 6 checks
  • Three objections, each written in the critic's voice at full strength
  • Each steelman would be recognised as fair by someone who holds that view
  • Each response classified as one of the three types above
  • At least one objection conceded — if none are, the steelmen are too weak
  • At least one objection is the kind an employer raises, not a reviewer
  • Delivered live, with a note on which question the lab asked that you had not anticipated

M6 Stewardship

Year 3+ · 4 skills

The loosest grouping of the six, and worth saying so plainly. What joins these four is that each produces something that outlasts you in this lab: a citable deposit, a dataset somebody can still find terms for in four years, an idea written well enough to be funded, and a person who learned to do research because you scoped their first task properly.

25

Zenodo deposit

arithmetic 2 hours prereq: 06-git-github, 07-code-folder-structure

A paper that says "code available on request" is a paper whose code will not be available in four years, because the student has graduated and the laptop is gone. A deposit makes it citable and permanent.

Turn in Zenodo record URL

Passes when — 4 checks
  • Record URL resolves and the archive contains the tagged release
  • Both DOIs present and placed per the standard
  • CITATION.cff parses — the GitHub "Cite this repository" button appears
  • The paper's data-availability statement cites the correct one of the two
26

Research data management

arithmetic 3 hours prereq: 25-zenodo

"Can this be deposited?" has to be answered before the deposit, because a deposit is public and permanent and the undo button does not fully exist. This lab's data makes the question live rather than theoretical: ERCOT data arrives under use agreements, industry partners supply material flows under NDA, and DOE-funded work can carry its own handling requirements.

Turn in one-page data statement

Passes when — 5 checks
  • Every dataset used in your work appears
  • Source named with the instrument it arrived under
  • Licence named explicitly per dataset
  • Any restriction quoted from the agreement, with the agreement located
  • Withheld items named, with a reason and a location
27

Proposal writing fragments

writing 6 hours prereq: 11-lit-review-gap, 15-imrad

Writing a full proposal is not a course assignment — it is a months-long effort with a real deadline attached to somebody's salary. But the two fragments that decide most proposals are each about a page, and both are learnable in isolation.

Turn in PDF, to the call's stated page limits

Passes when — 5 checks
  • A real call is named, with its deadline
  • Within the stated page limit — verified, not estimated
  • Required headings present exactly as the call words them
  • Broader impacts names an activity, an audience, and a measure
  • Intellectual merit traceable to the skill 10 gap statement
28

Mentoring an undergraduate

writing ongoing, plus 2 hours to write up prereq: 07-code-folder-structure

This lab runs three tiers, so PhD students supervise before anyone has asked whether they can. The characteristic failure is handing an REU student an unbounded task — "look into battery degradation models" — and reading it as the student's failure ten weeks later when nothing is finished.

Turn in task brief plus retrospective

Passes when — 6 checks
  • Task brief contains a definition of done that names an artifact
  • Input and output paths specified, consistent with the repo archetype
  • A checkpoint date, before the deadline
  • Retrospective names at least two underspecified items
  • The mentee's actual output is linked
  • If they used git, their .gitignore was verified before their first commit

  • The thesis: infrastructure has three layers

    Societies thrive or struggle on the infrastructure they build, and that infrastructure is never only physical. It spans three inseparable layers:

  • 1. Energy systems

    The system that keeps the lights on, and the system this lab models most.

  • 2. Critical mineral supply chains

    End to end and back. This phrase is the lab's central intellectual claim and it is worth understanding precisely.

  • 3. RFID and asset tracking

    The short version. RFID tags carry an identifier that a reader can interrogate without line of sight. That single property — no line of sight — is what separates it from a barcode and is the reason it works for pallets, hospital beds, and battery packs moving through a facility.

  • Numbers worth carrying

    Order-of-magnitude anchors for energy work. Not for precision — for catching a result that is wrong by a factor of a thousand, which is the failure that survives code review.

  • Working across borders

    The supply chains here cross a dozen jurisdictions with different rules, different data conventions, and genuinely different ideas about what a deadline means. The lab collaborates internationally and most of its students trained somewhere other than the United States.

ungraded Engineering Research Domain Knowledge

read later, once the domain is familiar

The questions behind the methods rather than inside them. What counts as evidence when you cannot run an experiment, and why a discount rate is a claim about how much future people matter.

  • Philosophy of science

    You build models of systems you cannot run experiments on. You will never have a control group for the Texas grid.

  • The normative content of your modelling choices

    A discount rate is a claim about how much future people matter. Three percent and seven percent are different moral positions, and the choice between them was argued out by economists and philosophers, not engineers.

  • Policy and institutional literacy

    You model ERCOT. Somebody decided ERCOT would be an energy-only market with no capacity payment, and that decision was political, recent, and revisable.

  • History of your own field

    The grid looks the way it does for reasons, and most of them are historical accidents that hardened. Rural electrification, PUHCA in 1935, the 1978 PURPA opening, restructuring in the 1990s, and Texas's decision to stay outside the interconnections.

  • The economics of what you model

    Externalities, market failure, and why a carbon price is a policy instrument rather than a moral statement. If your optimisation minimises cost, you have adopted a welfare framework — worth knowing which one, and what it does not capture.
