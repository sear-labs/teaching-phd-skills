## Why this exists

Most students arrive with a **topic** and believe it is a claim. "My research is about lithium
supply chains" is a topic. It cannot be argued with, which means it cannot be defended, which
means a committee has nothing to engage and will invent something instead.

The gap is not intelligence and it is not effort. It is that constructing an argument is taught
in first-year composition and in introductory logic, and an engineering degree taken outside
the United States contains neither. Nobody has ever shown these students the structure. They
are then asked, at a proposal defence, to perform it under pressure.

## External material

Booth, Colomb & Williams, *The Craft of Research*, chapters 7–10. This is the book. It is short,
it is written for exactly this gap, and chapters 7–10 are the argument core — claim, reasons,
evidence, acknowledgment and response, warrants. The library has it.

Stephen Toulmin's *The Uses of Argument* is the original source of the structure; you do not
need to read it, but the vocabulary below is his.

## Governing part

None.

## The mechanic

Take your own central finding and force it into one sentence:

> **I claim that X, because of Y, based on evidence Z.**

Then do the hard part. **Find the warrant.**

A warrant is the unstated general principle that licenses the jump from your reason to your
claim. It is almost never written down, and it is almost always where the argument actually
breaks. "Costs fell in the model, therefore the policy is good" carries a warrant — *that
system cost is the right measure of good* — which somebody will absolutely contest.

So: write the warrant out. Then ask whether a reasonable person in your field would grant it.
If they would not, you have found what your paper actually has to defend, and you have found it
while there is still time to do something about it.

Finally, list the **three strongest objections** to the claim. Not the easy ones.

## Deliverable

One page: the claim sentence, the warrant stated explicitly, and three objections.

## Competency check

- [ ] A single claim sentence with all three slots filled
- [ ] The claim is contestable — someone competent could disagree with it
- [ ] The warrant is stated as a general principle, not restated as the evidence
- [ ] Three objections, none of them trivial
- [ ] For each objection, one line on whether the paper currently answers it
