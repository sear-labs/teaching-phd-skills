## Why this exists

Most rejected drafts in this lab are not rejected for the research. They are rejected because
the reader cannot find the contribution — it is implied by the results section and stated
nowhere. IMRAD is not a formatting convention; it is a contract about **where a reader will
look for each thing**. A reader looking for your contribution will look in two places, and if
it is in neither, they conclude there isn't one.

## External material

The CCGA [IMRAD libguide](https://libguides.ccga.edu/researchbasics/imrad), already linked from
Report Draft 1. It covers what belongs in each section. It does not cover the contribution
claim, which is the mechanic and the only hard part.

## Governing part

None.

## The mechanic

Before any section gets written, one sentence:

> **This paper shows that ______, which matters because ______.**

Both blanks. The second is where most drafts fail — "which matters because it has not been done"
is not a reason.

Then one **topic sentence per section**, written before the section. Each must visibly serve the
claim. Then run the test that makes this worth doing:

**If a section's topic sentence does not serve the claim, either the section is wrong or the
claim is.** Decide which, in writing. Usually it is the claim, and discovering that now rather
than after drafting twelve pages is the entire return on this exercise.

## Deliverable

Overleaf link showing the claim sentence and the topic sentences, in the target template.

## Competency check

- [ ] The claim sentence exists, with both blanks filled
- [ ] "Matters because" gives a reason other than novelty
- [ ] One topic sentence per IMRAD section
- [ ] Each topic sentence traceable to the claim in one step
- [ ] Any section that failed the test is named, with the resolution stated
