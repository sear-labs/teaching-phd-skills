## Why this exists

Writing a full proposal is not a course assignment — it is a months-long effort with a real
deadline attached to somebody's salary. But the two fragments that decide most proposals are
each about a page, and both are learnable in isolation. The project summary is what a programme
officer reads to decide which panel sees it. Broader impacts is the section engineers routinely
treat as an afterthought and reviewers routinely score hardest, because a vague broader impacts
section is the clearest available signal that the rest was written in a hurry.

## External material

The [NSF PAPPG](https://www.nsf.gov/publications/pub_summ.jsp?ods_key=pappg) — Chapter II for
what the project summary must contain and the Intellectual Merit / Broader Impacts headings it
requires. A live DOE [funding opportunity
announcement](https://science.osti.gov/grants/FOAs) if your work is DOE-aligned, since the
format differs.

## Governing part

None.

## The mechanic

Work against a **real, currently-open call**. Named, with its deadline. A generic proposal
teaches nothing, because every constraint that makes proposal writing hard comes from the call.

1. **Project summary to the call's actual page limit**, with the headings the call requires. The
   limit is the exercise.
2. **Broader impacts naming three things**: a specific activity, a specific audience, and how
   you would know it worked. "Will broaden participation" fails all three. "Two REU students
   recruited through the UTA McNair programme, each producing a conference poster, tracked to
   graduate school application" passes.

Your gap statement from skill 10 is the raw material for the intellectual merit paragraph. If it
does not fit, the gap was not specific enough — go back to it.

## Deliverable

PDF, within the call's stated page limits.

## Competency check

- [ ] A real call is named, with its deadline
- [ ] Within the stated page limit — verified, not estimated
- [ ] Required headings present exactly as the call words them
- [ ] Broader impacts names an activity, an audience, and a measure
- [ ] Intellectual merit traceable to the skill 10 gap statement
