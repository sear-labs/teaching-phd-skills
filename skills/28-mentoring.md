## Why this exists

This lab runs three tiers, so PhD students supervise before anyone has asked whether they can.
The characteristic failure is handing an REU student an **unbounded** task — "look into
battery degradation models" — and reading it as the student's failure ten weeks later when
nothing is finished. It was not. An unbounded task given to someone without the context to bound
it themselves is a supervision failure, and it is entirely preventable by writing three things
down before the handoff.

## External material

[CIMER's Entering Mentoring](https://cimerproject.org/entering-mentoring/) curriculum — the
evidence-based standard for research mentor training, and the source most institutional
programmes draw from. It covers the relationship. The mechanic below covers the task, which is
where this specific failure lives.

## Governing part

**Part 1** governs the repo the mentee will be working in — including the ordering in skill 05,
which now applies to somebody who has never used git. Their first commit is your responsibility.

## The mechanic

Before the handoff, write:

1. **A definition of done.** Not "understand X" — a thing that exists when it is finished, which
   you would recognise on sight.
2. **A data path.** Exactly where the input is, and where output goes. This is where the
   archetype from skill 06 earns its keep.
3. **A checkpoint date** before the work is due, at which being off track is still recoverable.

Hand it off. Let them work.

Then the retrospective, which is the graded part: **name at least two things you left
underspecified.** You will find them. Everyone does. The list is the deliverable, because it is
the thing that makes the next handoff better, and it is what goes into the handbook for the
student who supervises after you.

## Deliverable

The task brief plus the retrospective, with the mentee's output linked.

## Competency check

- [ ] Task brief contains a definition of done that names an artifact
- [ ] Input and output paths specified, consistent with the repo archetype
- [ ] A checkpoint date, before the deadline
- [ ] Retrospective names at least two underspecified items
- [ ] The mentee's actual output is linked
- [ ] If they used git, their `.gitignore` was verified before their first commit
