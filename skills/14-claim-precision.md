## Why this exists

Reviewers reject papers for overclaiming more often than for bad work. The sentence *"our model
proves that recycling reduces import dependence"* will be attacked, and correctly, because a
model does not prove anything about the world — it shows what follows from its assumptions. The
same sentence with *shows*, scoped to the assumptions, is unattackable and says just as much.

This is the precision-of-language leg of the humanities, the thing composition courses drill and
engineering degrees never mention. It is also the cheapest fix in this entire curriculum: it is
a search-and-replace pass that measurably changes how your work is received.

## The mechanic

**Every claim verb encodes a strength.** Ranked, strongest first:

> proves · demonstrates · shows · indicates · suggests · is consistent with

A model run supports *shows* at best, and only inside its assumptions. Experimental replication
supports *demonstrates*. **Almost nothing in computational research supports *proves*** — that
word belongs to mathematics, and using it elsewhere tells a reviewer you do not know the
difference.

Then the second half, which matters more: **state the scope condition.** Not "recycling reduces
import dependence" but "under the 2035 demand trajectory and with collection rates above 60%,
recycling reduces import dependence." The scope condition is not a weakening. It is the part
that makes the claim true, and it pre-empts the objection rather than waiting for it.

**Audit your own draft.** Every sentence making a claim about the world: the verb used, what
your evidence actually licenses, and the revision. Then make the revisions.

### Then run the same audit on your CV

A CV is the same document type with a different audience: claims about the world, made in verbs,
which a reader will check. The failure mode is identical and more common. *Led* is not
*contributed to*. *Developed* is not *used*. *Designed* is not *ran*.

Two questions per bullet:

- **What does the verb claim, and can you evidence it?** If you were one of four people on a
  thing, "led" is a claim somebody will test in an interview and you will lose.
- **What is the scope condition?** "Built hourly load profiles" is stronger, not weaker, as
  "built hourly load profiles from public end-use data for ERCOT" — the specificity is what makes
  it checkable, and checkable is what makes it believed.

Overclaiming on a CV fails the same way it fails in a paper. The reader who catches one stops
believing the rest.

- [ ] Every claim-making sentence in the draft appears as a row
- [ ] Each row states what the evidence licenses, not just whether the verb "sounds strong"
- [ ] Every instance of *proves* is either removed or justified as genuine mathematics
- [ ] At least one claim gains an explicit scope condition

- [ ] The same audit run over every bullet of your own CV, with the revised CV attached
- [ ] At least one CV verb downgraded to what the evidence actually supports
