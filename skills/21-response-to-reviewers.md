## Why this exists

Rebuttals fail in two directions and both lose the paper. Defensive letters argue with the
reviewer and get rejected by an editor who reads tone before substance. Capitulating letters
accept every point including the wrong ones, and produce a worse paper that still gets
rejected — because a reviewer who was mistaken and got agreement now knows the authors were not
paying attention. The skill is holding a position with evidence while being genuinely easy to
work with.

This lesson pairs with your course structure directly: you receive a referee report on Report
Draft 1 and respond to it in Report Draft 2.

None worth assigning. Published advice on this is mostly anecdote.

## The mechanic

One table. Every reviewer point gets a row — including the ones you reject.

| Reviewer point | Response | Change made | Location |
|---|---|---|---|

Three rules that do the work:

- **Every point gets a row.** A point you skip reads as a point you could not answer.
- **Every change is located** by section or line number in the revised manuscript. "We have
  clarified this" without a location makes the editor go looking, and editors do not go looking.
- **At least one reasoned disagreement.** If you agreed with everything, either you got an
  unusually good review or you were not reading. Disagreement is stated as evidence, not as
  apology: "We retained the original formulation because [reason]; we have added a note at §3.1
  making the assumption explicit."

Tone check before submitting: **no apology that is not attached to a change.** "We thank the
reviewer" once at the top is enough.

## Deliverable

PDF response letter.

## Competency check

- [ ] Every reviewer point has a row
- [ ] Every claimed change is located by section or line
- [ ] At least one point respectfully declined, with a reason rather than an apology
- [ ] The revised draft actually contains the changes claimed — spot-checked against three rows
- [ ] No unattached apologies

## Log entry

Writing. The point you disagreed with, and whether you still think you were right.
