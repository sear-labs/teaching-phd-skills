## Why this exists

A paper sent to the wrong venue does not get rejected quickly — it gets desk-rejected after six
weeks, or sent to reviewers who evaluate it against criteria it was never trying to meet. That
is a lost semester. Two mechanics in particular are now failure points nobody warns students
about: **preprint policy** (posting to arXiv can disqualify you at some venues and is required
at others) and **AI-disclosure policy**, which most journals adopted between 2023 and 2025 and
which differ enough that you cannot assume.

## External material

The journals' own scope and author-guidelines pages — there is no substitute and no aggregator
that is reliably current. [Sherpa Romeo](https://v2.sherpa.ac.uk/romeo/) for preprint and
self-archiving policy across publishers.

## Governing part

None.

## The mechanic

Three candidate venues for a paper you are actually writing. For each, four things:

1. **Scope fit, in one sentence**, quoting the journal's own scope statement.
2. **Typical time to first decision.** Many journals publish this; where they do not, ask
   somebody who has submitted.
3. **Preprint policy.** Can you post it? Must you? Which version?
4. **AI-disclosure policy.** What must be declared, and where.

Then pick one and **justify it against the two you rejected** — not in the abstract, but for
this paper. "Higher impact factor" is not a justification.

Finally, draft the cover letter. First paragraph names the contribution, in the words from
skill 13.

## Deliverable

One-page memo plus the draft cover letter.

## Competency check

- [ ] Three named venues
- [ ] All four attributes present for each, with sources
- [ ] Choice justified against the two rejected, on grounds specific to this paper
- [ ] Cover letter states the contribution in its first paragraph
- [ ] AI-disclosure requirement identified and a draft disclosure written

## Log entry

Writing. Which venue you would have picked before doing this, and whether it changed.
