## Why this exists

"No one has done X" is not a gap. It is frequently evidence that X is not worth doing, and a
committee will say so. A gap is a specific thing that is **now possible and previously was
not** — because data became available, because a solver got faster, because a policy changed.
Students who cannot tell those apart write proposals that get rejected for a reason nobody
articulates clearly enough to fix.

## External material

None worth assigning. The database tutorials teach searching; nothing published teaches the
mechanic below well enough to substitute for it.

## Governing part

None.

## The mechanic

1. **Fifteen papers.** Fewer and the empty regions are artefacts of your sample.
2. **One axis.** Not a table of everything — one dimension along which the papers genuinely
   differ and which matters for your question. Spatial resolution. System boundary. Time
   horizon. Whether storage is co-optimised. Pick the one your contribution moves along.
3. **Plot them on it.** Literally. A scatter, a strip, a timeline — something with position.
4. **Find the empty region, then say why it is empty.** This is the step that does the work.
   Sometimes the answer is "nobody had the data until ERCOT published it in 2024." Sometimes it
   is "because it is not tractable and three people have tried." Only the first is a gap.
5. **Write the sentence:** *X is established, Y has not been tried, therefore Z.*

## Deliverable

PDF containing the map, the fifteen citations, and the gap paragraph.

## Competency check

- [ ] Fifteen papers, cited
- [ ] One named axis, with units or categories stated
- [ ] Every paper positioned on it
- [ ] The empty region identified
- [ ] One sentence on **why** it is empty, distinguishing "not yet possible" from "not worth doing"
- [ ] The three-part sentence, written out
