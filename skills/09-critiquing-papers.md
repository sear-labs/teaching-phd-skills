## Why this exists

Summarising is not critiquing, and the weekly log makes the difference easy to hide. A summary
restates what the authors claim. A critique tests whether the evidence they present actually
supports it. Students who have only ever summarised arrive at their own proposal defence unable
to say why a method was chosen over its alternative, because they have never once asked that
question of somebody else's paper.

## External material

already linked in WR1. It covers the form — summary, evaluation, reflection. It does not cover
how to evaluate evidence, which is the mechanic.

## Governing part

None.

## The mechanic

Three questions, in this order, on one paper close to your own work:

1. **What exactly is claimed?** Quote it verbatim. If you cannot find a sentence that states the
   claim, that is your first finding.
2. **What would have to be true for that claim to hold?** Name the load-bearing assumption. There
   is almost always exactly one that matters — a scale, a boundary condition, a dataset's
   representativeness.
3. **Does the paper show that?** Usually it shows something adjacent. Say which.

Then: what would you have done differently, **and what would it have cost?** The cost clause is
what separates a critique from a complaint. Every alternative is more expensive in some
dimension, and naming it is how you demonstrate you understood the constraint the authors were
under.

## Deliverable

WR entry, 300–500 words.

## Competency check

- [ ] The central claim is quoted verbatim, with a location
- [ ] One load-bearing assumption is named
- [ ] The gap between what is claimed and what is shown is stated explicitly
- [ ] A specific alternative is proposed, with its cost
